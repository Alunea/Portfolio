UX Analysis & UI Overhaul: Monster Hunter World

How do you modernize one of gaming’s most complex menu systems without losing its soul?
This collaborative project dives deep into information architecture and visual hierarchy.
We identified key pain points in the current user flow and proposed a complete UI redesign aimed at reducing cognitive load and enhancing accessibility for both veterans and newcomers.

Details

  • TYPE : Team Project
  • ROLE : UI/UX Designer
  • DATE : From Febuary 2026 to march 2026

My contributions

UX design

Immersive Analysis & Data Gathering

My primary focus was to bridge the gap between player frustration and design solutions.
After immersing myself in the game for several hours to identify its core complexities, I designed and deployed a comprehensive user survey.
I took care to craft questions with a neutral tone to eliminate bias and ensure the integrity of the data, focusing specifically on friction points within the HUD, navigation menus, and combat feedback.

Defining the Audience

Leveraging the quantitative and qualitative results from this survey, combined with secondary research on player archetypes,I developed the project’s User Personas.
These personas became our "north star," allowing us to categorize different player profiles and tailor the UI overhaul to solve their specific pain points.
By grounding our strategy in these insights, I ensured that every design iteration was a direct response to a real-world user need.​

Mapping the Experience

Building on the persona insights, I developed the User Journey Map to visualize the player's emotional arc and touchpoints during specific high-stakes tasks.
By pinpointing exactly where the "fun" was being interrupted by "friction," I could advocate for design changes that directly impact player retention and satisfaction.

Scientific Visual Audit

To provide an objective basis for our UI overhaul, I conducted a Cognitive Load Analysis on selected User Tasks.
This involved identifying information-heavy screens where the "visual noise" overwhelmed the player's ability to make quick decisions.
This analysis was the cornerstone of our final UI layout, ensuring we prioritized essential combat data while decluttering secondary information.

UI design

From Strategy to Structure: Wireframing

Once the friction points were identified, we translated our solutions into Low-Fidelity Wireframes. The goal was to reorganize the information architecture without the distraction of visuals.
We restructured the HUD elements to match the player’s natural visual flow, prioritizing survival data while decluttering secondary menus.

Interactive Prototyping in Figma

To validate our ideas, we built High-Fidelity interactive prototypes in Figma.
This allowed us to focus on the speed and responsiveness of the menus. We ensured that every action, from navigating the item pouch to swapping weapons, felt smooth and intuitive for the player.

Functionality & Accessibility

We also optimized the layout and font sizes to ensure the interface remained readable during intense combat.
These prototypes helped us simulate the final experience, ensuring the new UI was more functional and less intrusive while respecting the game's original logic.

Our creative process was highly iterative, we originally envisioned a horror game centered on phobias, specifically aquaphobia but ultimately pivoted toward a lighter, 'goofy' atmosphere to better suit our vision of fun.

Level Design

Regarding the Level Design process, once the team finalized the core mechanics, I took full ownership of Level 3-The Nightclub.

My role was to define the visual identity and atmosphere of this environment while crafting the entire level layout.
My primary design goal was to introduce a new gameplay mechanic, lasers, which required a careful balance of the difficulty curve.

Since the game’s unique propulsion controls already presented a challenge, I focused on a progressive learning path.
I started with low-risk encounters to let players familiarize themselves with the lasers, then gradually increased the stakes.
By balancing the complexity of the new hazards with the existing movement difficulty, I ensured that the level remained challenging yet fair, guiding the player through a smooth and rewarding progression.

Cube breaker

Cube Breaker is a one-button arcade game set in a vibrant neon universe. Master the power of fragmentation in an experience perfectly crafted for mobile gamers.
The core mechanic is simple yet deep: use a single tap to detonate your energy orb, splitting it into six smaller projectiles that blast outward in a lethal spray.
To shatter every cube on the screen, you must time your explosion at the perfect location to trigger the ultimate chain reaction.
Analyze the layout, find the sweet spot, and clear levels with the fewest attempts possible. In this world of neon and physics, precision is the key to total destruction.

My contributions

Game design

Cube Breaker is a solo project where I handled every aspect of production: Game Design, Programming, Level Design, and Game Feel.
The Core Constraint: One-Button Gameplay The primary challenge was to design a complete experience using only a single input.
This constraint immediately led me to the mobile platform, as "one-button" mechanics perfectly suit the intuitive nature of smartphone gaming.
From a programming perspective, I focused on technical feasibility while ensuring deep, engaging gameplay.
I drew my main inspirations from the physics-based puzzles of Cut the Rope and the precision of Pinball. Combined with my passion for neon aesthetics, I aimed to create a vibrant experience specifically tailored for casual gamers. The Iterative Design Process The project went through several iterations, starting with a comprehensive Game Concept Document (GCD).
During the prototyping phase, a major design shift occurred: I implemented a ball that moves continuously from right to left. This decision was crucial as it gave the player the power to choose their strategy with millimeter precision, transforming a simple click into a calculated tactical explosion

Programming

On the technical side, I developed the game using Unity’s Visual Scripting, focusing heavily on the robustness of the core mechanics.
A significant portion of the programming was dedicated to the ball’s fragmentation system, ensuring the trajectory of the six sub-projectiles felt natural while preventing technical issues like infinite bouncing loops.
I also programmed a variety of specialized cube behaviors, such as teleportation and shrinking, to keep the level design diverse and challenging.
Finally, I placed a major emphasis on Game Feel, which is essential for a casual mobile experience.
I implemented several 'juice' elements, including screen shakes, custom shaders, and polished particle effects, to make every explosion feel impactful and rewarding for the player.

Level design

Regarding the Level Design, my goal was to create a seamless onboarding experience through organic tutorialization.
I designed the initial stages to let players discover the explosion mechanic and the victory conditions naturally, without the need for intrusive text.
New elements, such as special-effect cubes, are introduced progressively to keep the learning curve smooth.
As the player advances, the number of targets increases, and the layouts become more complex to maintain a steady challenge.
However, I intentionally avoided a punitive design; the game encourages a 'relaxing yet engaging' flow where the player decides whether to restart a level to improve their score.
This non-mandatory retry system ensures that the experience remains accessible and stress-free, staying true to the casual mobile gaming philosophy.
