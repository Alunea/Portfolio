Make it melt

In Make It Melt, you play as Marceline, a skilled blacksmith whose raw materials have unexpectedly come to life and escaped. As these sentient ores are too tough to be broken by force, you must chase them through various levels and use strategy to guide them into the right furnaces. Heat is your only weapon in this frantic quest to reclaim your workshop’s supplies.

Mes Contributions

Game Design

This project was developed by a team of five Game Designers.
Since we handled the entire programming process ourselves, we had to be pragmatic: we adapted our design goals to our technical capabilities to ensure a polished and functional core experience.
Our goal was to deconstruct the traditional idea of a brawler.
Inspired by billiard physics, we moved away from pure aggression to incorporate a tactical and reflective layer. Players must anticipate trajectories and calculate angles, making strategy just as vital as the combat itself.

 While the Game Concept was a collaborative effort, I took full charge of the visual communication for our oral presentations. I designed and structured the PowerPoint decks, translating our collective ideas into a professional and impactful visual format to ensure our vision was effectively communicated during our pitches.  

Programmation

I co-managed the C# programming for this project using Unity.
My work focused on building the game's core architecture and ensuring a seamless player experience through three key areas:

  • Enemy  Behaviors:Engineered a diverse range of enemy behaviors using modular logic, including exploding types, jumping mechanics ...

  • Environment and Gameplay Mechanics: Programmed interactive level elements including platforms, door systems, ovens, and checkpoint logic. I also implemented the core player health system.​

  • UI/UX and Scene Management: Developed the complete menu system and programmed smooth scene transitions to ensure a professional flow throughout the game.

  • Juice and Game Feel: A significant portion of my contribution was dedicated to Game Feel. I implemented VFX (particle systems) and visual feedback to ensure that every interaction felt satisfying, responsive, and immersive.

Sploush

Sloupsh is a curious slime living in a peaceful city.
But for him, life doesn't end at the edge of the local neighborhoods.
Convinced that a vast world awaits beyond, he sets off on a journey. His only means of travel? A natural ability to propel himself using powerful water jets.

In this 2.5D platformer, the city becomes a massive playground for vertical exploration. Your survival and progress depend entirely on how you manage your water levels.every multi-directional blast consumes a part of your internal reservoir. Traverse the city from its deepest alleys to its highest peaks, and finally discover what lies beyond the walls.

Mes Contributions

Game design

Sloupsh was developed as a cross-curriculum team project, bringing together five Game Designers, two Programmers, and one Sound Designer.

Our creative process was highly iterative, we originally envisioned a horror game centered on phobias, specifically aquaphobia but ultimately pivoted toward a lighter, 'goofy' atmosphere to better suit our vision of fun.
Despite this shift, we chose to keep water as our core element, transforming it from a source of fear into an atypical and dynamic propulsion system.
Throughout the development, we had to balance our creative ambitions with technical feasibility, carefully managing our time and collective skill sets.

To stay organized, we relied on industry-standard tools like Trello and Excel.
As a Game Designer, I played a key role in the project's foundation, actively contributing to the creation of the Game Concept Document and the design of the various pitch presentations to ensure a clear and compelling vision for the team.

Level Design

Regarding the Level Design process, once the team finalized the core mechanics, I took full ownership of Level 3-The Nightclub.

My role was to define the visual identity and atmosphere of this environment while crafting the entire level layout.
My primary design goal was to introduce a new gameplay mechanic, lasers, which required a careful balance of the difficulty curve.

Since the game’s unique propulsion controls already presented a challenge, I focused on a progressive learning path.
I started with low-risk encounters to let players familiarize themselves with the lasers, then gradually increased the stakes.
By balancing the complexity of the new hazards with the existing movement difficulty, I ensured that the level remained challenging yet fair, guiding the player through a smooth and rewarding progression.
